FM 3-05.70 says snow shelters work because snow insulates, and that building one takes time and gets you wet. Do this only when you have a digging tool and enough snow. Do not dig into an avalanche slope; the site page covers that hazard.

## Snow trench

The manual's idea is to get below the snow and the wind. In compacted snow, cut blocks for a roof. If you cannot cut blocks, use a poncho or other sheet. Make one entrance and close it with a block or a pack.

## Snow cave in a drift

Find a drift about 3 meters (10 feet) deep. Keep the roof arched so it is stronger and so melt runs down the sides instead of onto you. Build the sleeping platform higher than the entrance. Leave a gap, or a small trench, between the platform and the wall so melt does not soak you. This matters more if you have a heat source inside. The roof should be high enough to sit up on the platform. Close the entrance with a block. The manual says you can use the lower entrance area for cooking. Walls and ceiling should be at least 30 centimeters (1 foot) thick. Put in a ventilation shaft. A sealed snow hole can smother you. Keep the shaft open.

## Mound you dig out

The same paragraph says that if you do not have a drift that deep, pile snow into a mound large enough to dig out. That mound-and-hollow shelter is the variation the manual gives. People often call a hollowed snow mound a quinzhee. The manual does not use that word and does not give a separate recipe, a wait time, or a diameter. Use the cave rules it does give: arched roof, sleeping platform above the entrance, thick walls and ceiling, and a ventilation shaft.

## Tree-pit

Where evergreen trees grow, the manual says you can dig the snow out around a bushy trunk down to the depth you need or to the ground, pack the snow at the rim, lay boughs across the top, and put boughs under you. The tree is a roof only if it is not a hazard tree. A dead or cracked tree is not a good pit.

In every one of these, insulate your body from the snow you are lying on. Bare snow under you steals heat even when the roof is good.

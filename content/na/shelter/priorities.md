Exposure can hurt you faster than hunger. The FEMA US&R survival appendix says shelter is the critical necessity in that short course: you can last longer without water than you can last while exposed to a harsh environment, and it prints a teaching mnemonic of about 3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter in hypothermia conditions, 3 days without water, and 3 weeks without food. Treat that as their planning line, not a promise about your body.

The same appendix says to work one problem at a time, and to keep the shelter small. A smaller space takes less heat to warm. Put dry, airy debris between your body and the ground. Heat lost into cold ground is a real loss. Plastic can be a moisture barrier under that insulation, not a substitute for it.

U.S. Army FM 3-05.70 says a shelter has to be large enough to protect you and small enough to hold your body heat, especially in the cold. It also says to prefer a natural shelter you can alter, because building from nothing spends energy you may need later.

Order of work when the weather is the threat:

- Get out of wind, rain, and standing water.
- Insulate yourself from the ground.
- Only then improve the roof, the door, and a way to signal.

Fire is not the first shelter. A fire you cannot keep inside a safe ring, or a fire under a snow-loaded branch, makes a second emergency. If you are with other people, one person can gather insulation while another picks a site that is not under a dead limb and not in a wash.

This page is not medical advice. Hypothermia care is on the first-aid page, and that page is still awaiting clinical review.

Use this when you have leaves, needles, grass, or similar dry debris and you need warmth more than room. Both the FEMA appendix and FM 3-05.70 describe it. The Army manual calls it one of the better shelters for warmth when shelter is essential.

Shape:

- Set a ridgepole with one end on a stump, log, or rock, or on a short tripod. The Army manual also allows anchoring the ridgepole to a tree at about waist height.
- Lean sticks along both sides to make a wedge. It must fit your body and be steep enough to shed water.
- Lay finer sticks and brush across the ribs so insulation does not fall through.
- Heap on light, dry, soft debris.

How thick: the FEMA appendix says to push your arm through the debris to the ribs and have debris reach your armpit, and in cold weather to add another foot or two. FM 3-05.70 says at least 1 meter (3 feet), and thicker is better. Use the thicker of the two if you have the material. The Army manual also says to put about 30 centimeters (1 foot) of insulation inside, on the floor, and to keep a pile you can pull across the door once you are in. A layer of branches or shingles on the outside keeps the debris from blowing off.

Do not build it under the hazards on the site-selection page. Dead limbs overhead and a wash underfoot are not fixed by more leaves.

If the debris is wet, it will not insulate the way the manuals describe. Spend the effort looking for drier material under blown-down trees or on the lee side of a log rather than piling soaked leaves and calling it done.

A lean-to blocks wind and rain from one direction. It is not a closed hut. FM 3-05.70 describes two versions.

## Poncho lean-to

The manual's materials are a poncho, about 2 to 3 meters (7 to 10 feet) of line, three stakes about 30 centimeters (1 foot) long, and two trees or poles about 2 to 3 meters apart. Check the wind first. The back of the lean-to faces the wind.

Tie the hood shut. Tie line to the two corner grommets on one long side, with a short stick on each line about 2.5 centimeters from the grommet so rain running down the line drips off instead of into the shelter. Tie those lines about waist high. Stake the other edge to the ground. For more than one night or for rain, add a center support so the roof does not sag into a puddle. Brush or a pack at the open sides cuts wind. Leaves or needles on the floor cut heat loss into the ground.

## Pole lean-to

In woods, the same manual describes a lean-to of poles: a horizontal support about waist to chest high between two trees, beams rested on one side, and the back into the wind. It takes longer than a poncho roof. In cold weather the manual describes a green-log reflector wall on the fire side, filled with dirt so it throws heat. Only build a fire where it is legal and where you can keep it from the shelter and the duff. The Park Service says sparks melt and ignite tents; the same is true of a thatched roof.

A lean-to under a dead limb is still a bad site. Read the site page before you tie the first knot.

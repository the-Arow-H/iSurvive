This is a stub. It does not add rainfall totals or a plant list beyond the cards that tag this biome.

The National Park Service camping page applies in these woods: look up for dead branches, stay out of ravines that flood, and do not camp on a peak or in the middle of a field in a lightning storm. Sequoia and Kings Canyon's hazard-tree warning is about western parks, and the same behavior is the right one under eastern hardwoods. Branches fall with or without wind.

Water is still treated. Clear creeks in deciduous forest are the kind of water CDC says can look safe and not be. Use the water module.

Plant cards tagged for this biome include white oak, black walnut, shagbark hickory, pawpaw, lowbush blueberry, and others. Open the plants module with this biome selected. Never eat on a single identification. The hemlock and yew rules are national, not regional.

Winter in the northern part of this forest is a shelter problem first. The debris hut and the lean-to are the builds that use leaves and a tarp. Snow shelters are for the boreal, alpine, and tundra stubs when the snow is deep enough for the Army methods.

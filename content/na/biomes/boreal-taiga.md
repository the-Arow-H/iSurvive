This is a stub for the northern conifer belt. It does not invent a temperature normal.

FM 3-05.70's snow trench, snow cave, tree-pit, and snow-mound variation are the shelter tools when the snow is deep. Keep a ventilation shaft in a cave or mound. Insulate your body from the snow. CDC says hypothermia also happens above 40°F when you are wet. The hypothermia page is a clinical draft. Read it as draft guidance and get help if someone is confused or shivering and exhausted.

Boil meltwater. The snowmelt page says not to eat raw snow, then to treat the water. Above 6,500 feet the boil is 3 minutes. Much of the boreal forest is below that elevation. Use the elevation you are actually at, not the biome name.

Plant cards that tag this biome include raspberry, lowbush blueberry, and nettle where the range statements overlap. Berries are seasonal. Conifer needles are not a tea in this guide. Yew is on the flora rules page because it is deadly.

Biting insects are a nuisance and a disease path. Ticks are covered on the bites page. Do not eat them.

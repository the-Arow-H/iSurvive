This is a stub for the humid Southeast and Gulf Coast. It does not add a hurricane procedure. If local authorities give an evacuation order, follow it. This guide is not a storm desk.

Heat is the NIOSH problem: heat stroke needs 911 and rapid cooling, and that page is a clinical draft. Humidity does not make the signs optional. Drink treated water on a schedule when you are working in the heat, and do not wait until you are confused.

The Park Service site rules still hold. Coastal and river country floods. Do not camp in a low spot because the ground is soft. Look up for dead limbs after storms. Widowmakers are not only a mountain problem.

Snakes are a NIOSH topic on the bites page. Do not cut a bite or put ice on it. Get emergency care. Alligators and other coastal hazards are not given a handling method here. Leave them alone.

Black cherry and pawpaw tag parts of this region in their FEIS ranges. Shagbark hickory is largely absent from the Gulf coastal plain in the FEIS review, so that card is not tagged here. Shellfish on the coast can carry toxins that cooking does not destroy. Read the fish page before you collect anything from a closed bed.

Water from a swamp still gets boiled or otherwise treated. CDC is clear that clear water can be unsafe, and swamp water is not even clear.

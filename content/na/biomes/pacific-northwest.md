This is a stub for the wet conifer forests of the Pacific Northwest. It does not add a rainfall number.

You will be wet. CDC says hypothermia happens above 40°F when rain or sweat soaks you. The hypothermia page is a clinical draft. Shelter priorities say dry ground insulation and a roof before a fire. A tarp lean-to with the back to the weather, and a debris hut if you can find dry litter under logs, are the builds.

Look up. The Park Service says not to camp under dead branches, and the Sequoia hazard page says trees fall in still air too. Big western trees make big widowmakers. Do not pitch in a debris-torrent channel.

Water is everywhere and still untreated. Boil it. The 3-minute rule is for elevations above 6,500 feet, which is a mountain number, not a rainforest number. Use your actual elevation.

Serviceberry and raspberry tag this biome where their FEIS ranges say so. Blue elderberry is the western riparian card from the USDA guide, with the red-berry warning. Never eat on a single identification. Mushrooms are not in this guide, including the ones that carpet these woods.

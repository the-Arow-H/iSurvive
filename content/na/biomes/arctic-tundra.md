This is a stub. Tundra does not offer a debris hut's worth of leaves. FM 3-05.70's snow trench, snow cave, and snow mound are the shelter shapes when snow is deep enough. Vent any cave. Insulate yourself from the snow. Wind is the exposure FEMA ranks ahead of food.

CDC's hypothermia page is the care reference and it is a clinical draft. Frostbite cautions on that page matter here: do not rub frozen skin, and do not thaw it on a stove.

Melt ice in preference to eating snow, then boil. Elevation still changes the boil time if you are above 6,500 feet.

CDC's trichinellosis outbreak report says freeze-resistant *Trichinella* is a northern problem and that freezing game, including meat frozen for weeks, did not make bear meat safe. Cook wild game to at least 165°F. That is the cooking page, not a suggestion to hunt. Follow territorial and state law. Alaska subsistence rules are not this card.

Dandelion is recorded in Alaska by FEIS. Most of the fruit and nut cards are not tundra plants. Do not force them. Never eat on a single identification. Polar bear liver and similar high-vitamin organ stories are not in the sources used here, so they are not repeated.

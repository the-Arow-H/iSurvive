This is a stub. The Park Service says not to stay on ledges or high peaks when wind and lightning are the hazard, and to stay off cliff edges. FM 3-05.70 says to avoid avalanche and rockslide paths. A snow cave dug in an avalanche path is not a shelter. It is a burial.

Above 6,500 feet, CDC says to boil water for 3 minutes, not 1. Much of the alpine Rockies is above that line. Melt snow before you drink it, then boil the melt. Do not eat raw snow.

Snow shelters in the Army manual need a ventilation shaft and a sleeping platform above the entrance. Tree-pits only work where the tree is alive enough not to be a hazard tree and bushy enough to help. Timberline may not give you that tree. Use the trench or the mound method if the snow allows, and do not invent a quinzhee diameter the manual does not print.

Serviceberry's FEIS range includes Colorado and Utah. That is not a reason to eat the first shrub with a dark fruit. Use the card. Hypothermia and heat both occur in the mountains, sometimes on the same day. Both care pages are clinical drafts.

This is a stub. FM 3-05.70's desert shade is the shelter: a sheet anchored on a rock outcrop or a sand mound, or a trench above the high-water mark with a roof of poles. Washes flood. Do not camp in them because the sky is clear when you lie down.

Heat illness is the medical risk NIOSH describes: heat stroke is an emergency, and you cool the person while you get help. That page is a clinical draft. Do not wait for sweat to stop before you take confusion seriously. NIOSH says heat stroke skin can be dry or still sweating.

Water you find in the desert is still water CDC says to treat, unless it is a posted potable tap. Springs are not automatically safe. The dew and sheet-still page is a small backup, and boiling is still the surest germ kill. Do not drink water that may hold metals or farm chemicals. Boiling will not fix that.

This edition's plant cards are thin in the hottest deserts on purpose. Raspberry reaches Arizona in the FEIS range, which is not the same as "every desert shrub is food." There is no cactus-eating card. An older FEMA appendix says all cactus is safe. This guide does not use that sentence.

Solar disinfection is a sunny-climate backup on the water page. It takes hours and clear bottles. It is not a reason to skip a boil you can do.

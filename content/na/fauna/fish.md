Fish is food only after it is cooked, and only if you may legally take it. Seasons, gear, and licenses are set by the state or province. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service says that in most cases you need a hunting or fishing license from the state where you are, and that you have to follow that state's rules. This page does not list those rules.

USDA FSIS says cook fish and shellfish to a minimum internal temperature of 145°F (62.8°C). FoodSafety.gov says the same 145°F (63°C) for fish, or cook until the flesh is no longer translucent and separates with a fork. Use a food thermometer if you have one. Color alone is a weak test.

CDC's anisakiasis page says not to eat raw or undercooked fish or squid. People get the parasite from those dishes. The prevention line there is to cook seafood to an internal temperature of at least 145°F (about 63°C). Freezing can kill parasites under the conditions CDC prints: −4°F (−20°C) or below for 7 days, or −31°F (−35°C) until solid and then held at that temperature for 15 hours, or −31°F until solid and then −4°F for 24 hours. A camp cooler is not those conditions. Cook the fish.

Shellfish are a different problem. CDC's Yellow Book says marine shellfish toxins, including the toxin in paralytic shellfish poisoning, are not destroyed by stomach acid, canning, cooking, freezing, pickling, salting, or smoking. Avoid shellfish from waters during or just after harmful algal blooms, including what people call red tide. Cooking will not fix that. If a state has closed a beach or a shellfish bed, stay out of it.

Gut fish away from camp water, and treat the water you wash with. The water pages still apply.

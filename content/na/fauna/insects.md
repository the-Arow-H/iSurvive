Insects are not a recommended food in the public pages this guide relies on. A FEMA training appendix says insects are edible and that flesh should be cooked. That line is broad enough to be dangerous, and this guide does not repeat it as permission. There is no USDA food-safety page in the sources here that names a North American insect species as a safe ration.

Do not eat ticks. CDC's tick page is about disease, including the need to remove attached ticks promptly. Tularemia prevention includes avoiding tick and deer-fly bites. A tick is a vector, not a snack. Do not eat flies off a carcass, and do not eat insects found on a sick or dead animal.

If you are considering any insect despite that, this card still has no species list and no "bright color" rule, because those rules are not in the cited pages. Cooking temperatures on the FSIS chart are for fish, poultry, and meat, not for a named insect. Do not invent a temperature and call it USDA.

Spend the effort on the plant cards you can actually match, or on getting help. Hunger is real. A wrong insect is not the fix this edition will pretend to teach.

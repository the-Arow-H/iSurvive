Use a food thermometer in the thickest part. These are the public numbers, including where they are not identical.

## The charts

USDA FSIS safe minimums:

- Fish and shellfish: 145°F (62.8°C).
- All poultry, including ground poultry and whole birds: 165°F (73.9°C).
- Ground meats (beef, pork, lamb, veal on that chart): 160°F (71.1°C).

FoodSafety.gov adds rabbit and venison, wild or farm-raised, at 160°F (71°C). It matches fish at 145°F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes.

## Wild game and Trichinella

CDC says the way to prevent trichinellosis is to cook meat to a safe internal temperature and not to sample it while it is raw. The animals it names as a risk include bear, wild boar, wildcat, fox, wolf, seal, and walrus, and undercooked pork. Curing, drying, smoking, or microwaving alone does not reliably kill the worms. Freezing pork has a CDC method (less than 6 inches thick, 20 days at 5°F / −15°C). Freezing wild game may not work, because some worms in wild game are freeze-resistant.

CDC's report on the 2022 bear-meat outbreak says cooking wild game to at least 165°F (74°C) is necessary to kill *Trichinella*, that the meat had been frozen for weeks and was still infectious, and that color is not a test of doneness. Vegetables cooked in the same pan as the rare meat also made people sick. Keep raw meat and its juice off other food.

Where FoodSafety.gov says 160°F for rabbit and venison and CDC's outbreak report says 165°F for wild game, this guide uses 165°F for wild mammals. Missouri's rabbit page also says at least 165°F because of tularemia. Poultry stays at the FSIS 165°F. Fish stays at 145°F, which is also CDC's anisakiasis cooking temperature.

## What cooking does not fix

Shellfish toxins are not destroyed by cooking or freezing. That is the CDC Yellow Book line on the fish page. A sick animal is not made safe by a higher number. Do not eat it.

No antibiotic and no antiparasitic dose belongs on this page. If you are sick after a meal, get medical care and say what you ate.

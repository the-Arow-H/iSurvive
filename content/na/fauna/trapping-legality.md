Trapping and hunting are legal only inside the rules of the place you are standing. Those rules are not the same in Maine and Arizona, and they are not the same in a Canadian province as in the state across the border.

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service says that in most cases you need a hunting license from the state where the hunt happens, and you must follow that state fish and wildlife agency's requirements. Licenses are sold through state systems and many sporting-goods outlets. Every state writes its own seasons. Look them up before you go. A license from last year, or from a different state, is not a guess you should test.

On national wildlife refuges, the Service says trapping has to follow state laws, state regulations, and state licensing, and some refuges also require a special-use permit. Some refuges do not allow public trapping even when state land nearby does. Alaska subsistence rules are a separate statute. Read the refuge sheet, not a forum post.

This page does not describe how to build a trap, a snare, or a deadfall. Those methods are regulated as equipment. Describing a trap here would not tell you whether it is legal in your county this month. If you trap, use the current regulations booklet from the state or province, take the required education course, and use only the devices that booklet allows.

Provinces run their own systems. A U.S. federal page will not answer a provincial season. Check the provincial wildlife agency.

Poaching is not a survival technique this project will write down. If you are lost, the shelter and water pages are the immediate work. Food can wait longer than exposure.

## Identification

FEIS describes common persimmon as a slow-growing, thicket-forming, dioecious deciduous tree, up to 70 feet (21 m) and generally under 40 feet (12 m), with a rounded or conical crown. Leaves are simple, alternate, entire, and elliptical to oblong. The fruit is a persistent spherical berry with one to eight flat seeds. Flowers bloom from March onward on the seasonal note in that review. Match leaf, fruit, and growth form together.

## Season

FEIS says ripe fruit can be eaten. It mentions unripe fruit only in a medicinal context this guide will not teach. Wait until the fruit is ripe. If it is still hard and astringent, it is not ready.

## Range

Use the FEIS review and the USDA PLANTS map for the stand in front of you. The species is an eastern U.S. tree in that review's community lists. This card does not add a state list the extracted review section did not print as a clean range sentence.

## Preparation

FEIS says ripe fruit can be eaten raw, cooked, or dried. Spit out or remove the seeds. They are not described as food. The review's notes on unripe fruit and inner bark for fever, diarrhea, and hemorrhage are medicinal and are not instructions here.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

The FEIS review used here does not name a deadly look-alike. That is not a clearance. Never eat this plant on a single identification. Eat only ripe fruit from a tree that matches the leaf and berry above. Do not eat seeds, bark, or unripe fruit from this card. If the fruit is not the persistent spherical berry on this tree, leave it.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

## Identification

FEIS describes lowbush blueberry as an erect, low, variable shrub about 2 to 24 inches (5 to 60 cm) tall, usually in dense colonies. Roots are shallow. Flowers are in short racemes, terminal or axillary. The fruit is a globular berry about 0.12 to 0.4 inch (4 to 11 mm) across, larger on some cultivars, and FEIS calls the berries very sweet.

## Season

Pick when the berries are ripe and sweet. FEIS does not give one month for the whole range.

## Range

FEIS says it grows from Labrador and Newfoundland west to southern Manitoba and Minnesota, south to northern Illinois, and from New England through the Appalachians to West Virginia and Virginia. The state list on that review includes CT, DE, IA, IL, IN, ME, MA, MI, MN, NH, NJ, NY, NC, OH, PA, RI, TN, VT, VA, WI, and WV. A blueberry outside that pattern needs a different identification. Do not stretch this card to every blue berry in the West.

## Preparation

FEIS says Native Americans ate the fruit fresh, dried, baked, in soups, or mixed with meat, and that settlers ate it fresh or in jams, jellies, and preserves. Wash the berries. No leaching step is described for the ripe fruit.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

The FEIS review used here does not name a deadly look-alike. That is not a clearance. Never eat this plant on a single identification. The plant has to be the low colony-forming shrub with the berry FEIS describes. A blue berry on a tall plant, or a berry with a different calyx, is not this card.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

## Identification

The USDA NRCS plant guide (symbol FRVI) describes *Fragaria virginiana* as a herbaceous perennial in the rose family. It spreads by seed and by short rhizomes and leafless stolons. Leaves are toothed, thin, and basal, with a petiole generally 1 to 12 cm, in threes, and generally smooth above. Flowers have 5 white petals about 4 to 9 mm, numerous pistils, and 20 to 35 stamens. The five bractlets are unlobed. The fruit is red and fleshy, with achenes on the surface.

## Season

Eat ripe red fruit. The guide does not give one month for the whole range. Flowers and fruit are not interchangeable. The guide notes that plants with bigger flowers may have less fruit.

## Range

The guide says to check the PLANTS profile for the current map. The copy used here says the plant is found between about 1,200 and 3,300 m in meadows and forest openings, in the Klamath Ranges, high Cascade Range, Modoc Plateau, and Sierra Nevada, and to eastern North America. A strawberry in a city lawn may be a different cultivated plant. Match the features anyway.

## Preparation

The guide says Indigenous peoples through parts of Canada picked and ate the fruit, and names Midwestern prairie and Great Lakes tribes, including the Omaha, Hidatsa, and Mandan, among people who used it. Wash the fruit and eat it ripe. Leaves are not described as the food on this card.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

The USDA guide used here does not name a deadly look-alike. That is not a clearance. Never eat this plant on a single identification. You want all of these together: three leaflets, five white petals, unlobed bractlets, and a red fruit with the seeds on the surface. If the fruit is dry, white, or held differently, do not eat it from this card.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

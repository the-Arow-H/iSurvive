## Identification

The USFS FEIS review describes common dandelion as an introduced cool-season perennial. It has a thick taproot. Stems are very short and underground, so the leaves form a rosette on the ground. Leaves are about 2 to 16 inches (5 to 40 cm) long. Flower heads are solitary at the end of naked, hollow stalks that can reach about 2 feet (60 cm). One head can hold 100 to 300 flowers. Seeds sit under a parachute of bristles.

## Season

FEIS does not print a month-by-month harvest chart. It is a cool-season plant, and the food note below is about the leaves. Do not assume a calendar the review does not give.

## Range

FEIS says it is naturalized throughout the United States, in all 50 states, almost all Canadian provinces, and Mexico. It is an invader of overgrazed range. Do not harvest where spraying is likely, such as a treated lawn. This guide has no test for herbicide residue.

## Preparation

FEIS says Gwich'in Athabaskan people of Fort Yukon, Alaska, frequently eat the leaves in salads or boil and eat them. Tea and wine from the flowers are also recorded there. The same paragraph says roots can be ground as a mild laxative or for heartburn. This guide does not teach that medicinal use. Eat leaves only, and only from plants that match the identification above. Wash them.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

The FEIS review used here does not name a deadly look-alike. That is not a clearance. Never eat this plant on a single identification. The features that have to be present together are the ground rosette, the naked hollow stalk, and the single head. A stalk with leaves on it is not the plant FEIS describes. Do not eat the root from this card.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

## Identification

FEIS describes chokecherry as a native deciduous shrub or small tree that forms thickets. Stems are numerous and slender. Height is often about 3 to 19.5 feet (1 to 6 m), sometimes taller. Flowers are on leafy twigs of the season. Fruits are drupes, each with a small stone.

## Season

FEIS says the fruits are somewhat astringent and relatively sweet when fully ripe. Use fully ripe fruit. Unripe fruit is not the food described there.

## Range

FEIS treats chokecherry as a widespread native shrub and discusses it in eastern, central, and western communities, including prairie and mountain plantings. Use the FEIS review and the USDA PLANTS map for the stand in front of you rather than a state guess from this card. The food rule does not change with the state: ripe flesh only.

## Preparation

FEIS says fruits are used to make wines, syrups, jellies, and jams, and that Indigenous peoples gathered them. Remove the stones before you cook. Do not blend whole fruit. Do not eat the seeds.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

USDA ARS lists chokecherry (*Prunus* spp.) among plants that contain cyanide (prussic acid). Leaves, bark, and seeds are not food. Never eat this plant on a single identification. Black cherry is a related tree with the same limit, and it is a separate card. If you cannot tell them apart, you can still follow the shared rule, and you should not invent a third use. If the fruit is not a ripe drupe on this thicket-forming shrub, do not eat it.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

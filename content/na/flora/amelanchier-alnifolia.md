## Identification

FEIS describes Saskatoon serviceberry as a native deciduous shrub or small tree, about 3 to 26 feet (1 to 8 m), often in thickets or clumps. Flowers and fruits are in terminal clusters. The fruits are berrylike pomes. Each fruit has 4 to 10 small seeds, and the seedcoat is leathery.

## Season

Use ripe fruit. FEIS does not give a single calendar date. Unripe fruit is not what the food notes describe.

## Range

FEIS says the species runs from east-central California north to southern Alaska, east to Ontario and southwestern Quebec, and south to southern Colorado and Utah. Varieties split that map. The typical variety is mostly east of the Cascades from southern Alaska to southern Oregon, and east to the Dakotas, Nebraska, and Colorado. Check PLANTS for the plant in your hand. Other *Amelanchier* species are not automatically this card.

## Preparation

FEIS says the fruits are added to pastries and used for jelly or syrup, and that several cultivars are grown for fruit. Wash the pomes and cook them if you are not sure they are fully ripe and clean. The review also says Native Americans made a medicinal tea by boiling the branches. This guide does not teach that tea.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

The FEIS review used here does not name a deadly look-alike. That is not a clearance. Never eat this plant on a single identification. You want the clustered pomes on this shrub, not a lone berry on a different plant. If you cannot match the cluster and the growth form, do not eat it.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

## Identification

FEIS describes American red raspberry as a deciduous, erect or arching, thicket-forming shrub about 1.6 to 9.8 feet (0.5 to 3 m) tall. Woody stems are bristly or prickly, with shreddy yellow-brown bark. Leaves are alternate and pinnately compound, with three to five leaflets, green above and white or gray and hairy underneath. The native North American plant in that review is *Rubus idaeus* subsp. *strigosus*.

## Season

Eat fruit when it is ripe on the plant. FEIS says it was eaten fresh or preserved for winter. It does not print one ripening month for the whole range.

## Range

FEIS says it grows from Alaska through Canada to Newfoundland, south to North Carolina and Tennessee in the East, and to Arizona, California, and northern Mexico in the West. Cultivated plants have naturalized as well. A garden raspberry is the same species complex. A wild cane still has to match the features above.

## Preparation

FEIS says the fruit was traditionally an important food, eaten fresh or preserved, and that the fruit is eaten fresh or in jams, jellies, and baked goods. Wash it. You do not need a special detox step for ripe fruit of this species in that review. Do not eat the leaves from this card. The review mentions medicinal preparations from fruit, root bark, and stems. This guide does not teach those.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

The FEIS review used here does not name a deadly look-alike. That is not a clearance. Never eat this plant on a single identification. The fruit has to be the aggregate cluster on a bristly cane with the leaf shape above. A juicy berry on a thornless plant with different leaves is not this card. If any feature fails, leave it.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

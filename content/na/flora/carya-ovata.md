## Identification

FEIS describes shagbark hickory as a medium to large deciduous tree, commonly 60 to 80 feet (20 to 25 m). Open-grown crowns are oblong. Forest trees tend toward a straighter, narrower crown. The gray bark exfoliates in long plate-like strips. That shag is the feature the common name is for. The tree is monoecious. The fruit is the nut. A smooth-barked hickory is not this species.

## Season

Collect mature nuts. FEIS does not give a single calendar date for the whole range.

## Range

FEIS says shagbark hickory occurs through most of eastern North America but is largely absent from the southeastern and Gulf coastal plains and the lower Mississippi Delta. It runs from southeastern Nebraska and southeastern Minnesota east through southern Ontario and Quebec to Maine, and south to Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana, and eastern Texas, with disjunct populations reported in the mountains of northeastern Mexico.

## Preparation

FEIS says the nuts are sweet and edible, that they were a staple for some Native American peoples, and that they are the commercial hickory nut. Crack and eat the kernel. Discard insect-bored or moldy nuts. The bark plates are not food.

## DEADLY LOOK-ALIKES

The FEIS review used here does not name a deadly look-alike. That is not a clearance. Never eat a nut on a single identification. Other hickories grow in the same woods and are not automatically sweet. If the bark does not shed in the long plates FEIS describes, do not use this card. A nut you cannot match to the tree stays on the ground.

## Rule

Never eat on a single identification. When in doubt, do not eat it.

**NOT MEDICAL ADVICE.** The steps below are from NIOSH's heat-illness page for workers. They apply to anyone overheating outdoors.

## Heat stroke

NIOSH says heat stroke is the most serious heat illness. The body can no longer control its temperature. Temperature can rise to 106°F or higher within 10 to 15 minutes. It can cause permanent disability or death if treatment is delayed.

Signs on that page: confusion, altered mental status, slurred speech, loss of consciousness, hot dry skin or profuse sweating, seizures, very high body temperature.

What NIOSH says to do:

- Call 911.
- Stay with the person.
- Move them to shade or a cool place and remove outer clothing.
- Cool them quickly. A cold-water or ice bath if you can. Wet the skin. Cold wet cloths. Soak clothing with cool water.
- Fan them.
- Cold wet cloths or ice on the head, neck, armpits, and groin, or soak the clothing.

## Heat exhaustion

Signs: headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, irritability, thirst, heavy sweating, elevated body temperature, less urine.

NIOSH says to get the person to a clinic or emergency room, and to call 911 if medical care is not available. Have someone stay with them. Move them out of the heat, remove extra clothing including shoes and socks, cool them with cold compresses or by washing the head, face, and neck with cold water, and encourage frequent sips of cool water.

## Other heat problems on the same page

Heat cramps: muscle cramps or spasms. NIOSH says drink water and have a snack or a drink that replaces carbohydrates and electrolytes, such as a sports drink, every 15 to 20 minutes, and to avoid salt tablets. Get medical help if the person has heart problems, is on a low-sodium diet, or the cramps last more than an hour.

This guide does not turn "a sports drink" into a recipe or a salt dose.

Rhabdomyolysis can follow hard work in heat. NIOSH lists muscle pain, weakness, and urine the color of tea or cola. Stop, drink water, and get to medical care. Do not wait that one out in camp.

Heat rash: cool off, keep the skin dry. NIOSH says not to use ointments and creams on it.

Confusion plus a very hot body is heat stroke until a clinician says otherwise. That is an evacuate-now problem.

**NOT MEDICAL ADVICE.** This is not a diagnosis, a prescription, or a wilderness-medicine course. If someone is badly hurt, confused, having trouble breathing, or getting worse, contact local emergency services. In much of the United States and Canada that number is 911. Poison Control in the United States is 1-800-222-1222.

Every page in this module is marked `review: pending-clinical`. A licensed pharmacist, the owner of this project, does a clinical last look before any first-aid page is treated as ready to ship. Until that review is written onto the page, read it as a draft that repeats public agency wording.

What these pages will not do:

- They will not tell you a drug dose. There are no milligram amounts for pain medicine, antibiotics, or any other drug.
- They will not copy Stop the Bleed or Red Cross course manuals. Those programs are linked so you can take the class.
- They will not replace hands-on training. Bleeding control, CPR, and splinting are skills you practice before you need them.

What to do while you wait for help, from the public pages cited on the topic pages: stop dangerous bleeding with firm pressure, get a cold person out of wet clothes and wind, cool a person in heat stroke, and do not straighten a deformed limb. The evacuate page lists situations those same sources treat as emergencies.

**NOT MEDICAL ADVICE.** "Evacuate" here means get to emergency care, or get care to you. It does not mean walk out if walking will kill you. Call first when you have a signal. Move a person only to get them out of further danger, which is the Ready.gov order.

Go now, or call now, if any of these are true. Each line is from the page cited.

- Bleeding that spurts or that you cannot stop with firm direct pressure. Ready.gov treats that as a reason to keep pressure until EMS arrives, and to use a tourniquet on an arm or leg when pressure will not stop it.
- Hypothermia signs, or a measured temperature below 95°F. CDC calls that an emergency. An unconscious cold person needs emergency help even if you are not sure you feel a pulse.
- Heat stroke signs: confusion, seizure, loss of consciousness, or a very high temperature. NIOSH says call 911 and cool the person while you wait. Heat exhaustion also goes to a clinic, and to 911 if no clinic exists.
- Any venomous-snake bite. NIOSH says do not wait for symptoms.
- A burn that is larger than about two inches, heavily blistered, leathery or patchy white, brown, or black, or caused by chemicals or electricity. Ready.gov.
- A limb that is bent wrong, has bone showing, has lost feeling or a pulse, or has blue numb fingers or toes. MedlinePlus.
- A tick bite followed by rash or fever. CDC says see a doctor. That can often wait for the next clinic rather than a night march. Do not invent an antibiotic plan in camp.
- A bite or scratch from a bat, raccoon, skunk, fox, or other wild mammal, or contact with a bat when a bite may have gone unnoticed. CDC says seek medical care urgently.

Do not start a long walk at the end of the day through terrain you cannot see, into a storm, or across a flood wash, if you can signal from where you are. The shelter and water pages still apply while you wait. Food is not the reason to move if the problems above are the reason you are moving.

Tell the responders what happened, what you did, and the time you put on a tourniquet if you used one. Do not take the tourniquet off to "check."

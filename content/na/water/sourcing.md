CDC's hiking page says germs from human or animal waste get into lakes, rivers, and streams, and that water can look clean and still be unsafe. Drinking straight from a clear mountain stream is given there as an example of something that is generally not safe. You can get sick from using untreated water to drink, cook, wash produce, make ice, or brush your teeth.

People at higher risk include children, pregnant people, adults 65 and older, and anyone with a weakened immune system. The illness CDC names from this kind of water is often diarrhea, vomiting, and stomach pain.

Boiling is the best way CDC gives to kill germs. The next best, on that page, is to filter and then disinfect the filtered water. Follow the label on any device you use.

If the water may contain fuel, toxic chemicals, or radioactive material, CDC says boiling and disinfecting will not make it safe. Use a different source. That includes flood water you cannot see through a chemical lens, and water from a radiator or boiler. The emergency page says never to use heating-system water for drinking, cooking, or hygiene.

Where you take water matters for the next person. If there is no toilet, CDC says to bury feces at least 8 inches deep and at least 200 feet from lakes, rivers, and other natural water, downstream of where people collect. Wash hands before food and after the toilet. If you have no soap and water, CDC says a sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol.

The boil, chemical, filter, and sun pages are the treatment steps. Do not skip them because the source "looks spring-fed."

A spring is groundwater coming to the surface. That sounds cleaner than a muddy creek. CDC does not give springs a pass. The hiking page says water that looks clean can hold bacteria, viruses, and parasites, and uses a clear mountain stream as the example. Treat spring water, seep water, and stream water the same way unless a land manager has posted that a specific tap or fountain is potable.

Prefer the clearest water you can reach, then make it clearer before treatment. CDC's emergency page says if water is cloudy, pour it through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee filter, or let it settle and draw off the clear water. Then boil, or disinfect, or filter according to the method pages. Cloudy water blocks chemical treatment and sunlight.

Do not take water downstream of a carcass, a latrine, or an obvious chemical sheen. CDC says you cannot boil fuel or toxic chemicals out of water. Move upstream or use a stored supply.

In camp, keep the container you treat into clean, and cover it. The emergency page says to store boiled or disinfected water in clean, sanitized containers with tight covers.

This page does not list liters per day. The older kit notes in this repository use a planning floor that is not on the CDC pages cited here, so it is not repeated as a medical requirement.

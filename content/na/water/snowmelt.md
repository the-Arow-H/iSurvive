The FEMA appendix says eating raw snow can cause dehydration, and that ice is preferable to snow. Melt ice or snow in a pot over a fire or stove. Do not use body heat as the melter if you are already cold: that is the opposite of the shelter priority, which is to stop heat loss.

Meltwater is not automatically safe. Snow and ice sit where animals walk and where dust and soot land. CDC says clear water can still carry germs, and the emergency page's boil steps apply to water you have made clear. If the melt is cloudy, settle or strain it, then bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 feet.

Do not melt snow that is stained with fuel, dye, or unknown waste. CDC says boiling does not make fuel or toxic chemicals safe.

Yellow snow is not a joke and not a source. Pick clean snow away from camp trails and latrines, then still treat it.

CDC calls boiling the best way to kill germs in water, including viruses, bacteria, and parasites such as Cryptosporidium and Giardia.

If the water is cloudy, strain it through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee filter, or let it settle and pour off the clear water.

Then:

1. Bring the clear water to a rolling boil for 1 minute.
2. Above 6,500 feet (about 1,981 meters), boil for 3 minutes.
3. Let it cool so you do not burn yourself.
4. Store it in a clean, covered container.

Boiling does not remove fuel, toxic chemicals, or radioactive material. If that is the problem, CDC says to use a different source.

A flat taste is normal. CDC says you can pour the water back and forth between clean containers and let it stand, or add a pinch of salt per quart or liter. That pinch is for taste, not a medical dose.

Boil water you will use for drinking, cooking, and brushing teeth when the only supply is untreated surface water. The hiking page says to boil even if you are not sure, and that filtering alone is not the first choice.

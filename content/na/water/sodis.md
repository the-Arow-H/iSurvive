CDC's emergency page says the sun can reduce the number of germs in water. It also says solar disinfection does not work well when the water is cloudy, because particles shade the germs.

Steps on that page:

1. If the water is cloudy, strain it through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee filter, or let it settle and draw off the clear water.
2. Fill clean, clear plastic bottles with that clear water.
3. Lay the bottles on their side in the sun for 6 hours if it is sunny, or 2 days if it is cloudy. Laying them down exposes more water to the light. A dark surface under the bottles helps.

This is a backup when you cannot boil. It is slow, and it does nothing for fuel, toxic chemicals, or radioactive material. CDC says those waters need a different source.

Do not use a bottle that held chemicals. "Clear" means you can see through the plastic and the water. Colored bottles are not what the page describes.

Boiling remains the method CDC calls best. Use the sun when boiling is impossible, not as a shortcut past a stove you already have.

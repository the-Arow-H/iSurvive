CDC's emergency page says portable filters do not remove all germs.

- Viruses: a typical portable filter will not remove them.
- Bacteria: the filter needs an absolute pore size of 0.3 micron or smaller.
- Parasites such as Giardia and Cryptosporidium: absolute pore size of 1 micron or smaller.

The hiking page says a filter labeled either of these removes parasites but not viruses, and not all bacteria:

- absolute pore size of 1 micron or smaller, or
- certified to NSF standards 53 or 58 for cyst reduction or removal.

A 0.3 micron absolute pore size removes bacteria and parasites, not viruses. Reverse osmosis filters remove parasites, bacteria, and viruses, and also remove salt.

After filtering, CDC says to add a disinfectant (iodine, chlorine, or chlorine dioxide) so viruses and remaining bacteria are killed. The hiking page calls filter-then-disinfect the next best option after boiling. Boiling is still the surest single step.

Follow the manufacturer's instructions. A clogged or frozen filter does not meet the pore size you bought. Do not assume a coffee filter or a bandana is a germ filter. CDC lists cloth only as a way to remove cloudiness before the real treatment.

Filters do not make fuel or chemical contamination safe.

# Disclaimer

**Not medical advice. Not legal advice. Not a rescue service.**

iSurvive is a field reference. It is not a doctor, a pharmacist's prescription,
a wilderness-medicine course, or a substitute for emergency care. If someone
is badly hurt, confused, not breathing normally, or getting worse, contact
local emergency services. In the United States and Canada that is often 911.
Poison Control in the United States is 1-800-222-1222.

The first-aid pages repeat public guidance from CDC, Ready.gov, NPS, NIH
MedlinePlus, and public Red Cross pages, and they link to Stop the Bleed.
They do not give drug doses. Every first-aid page is marked
`review: pending-clinical` until a licensed clinician finishes a last look.
The owner of this project is a licensed pharmacist and does that review
before anything is treated as ready to ship. Until that review is recorded
on the page, read the page as a draft.

Plant and animal pages can be wrong in the field even when the sources are
right. A plant that is safe in a book can be the wrong plant in your hand.
Never eat a wild plant on a single identification. This guide does not cover
mushrooms. Poison hemlock, water hemlock, death camas, and yew are included
as plants to avoid, not as food.

Trapping, hunting, and fishing rules change by state and province. A method
described here can be illegal where you are. Read the current regulations
before you take wildlife.

Water and shelter instructions are paraphrases of public agency pages.
They omit figures from older pamphlets that conflict with current CDC water
guidance. Boiling times in this guide follow CDC: 1 minute at a rolling boil,
or 3 minutes above 6,500 feet (about 1,981 meters).

No page in this guide promises an outcome. Cold, heat, bad water, a wrong
plant, and a delay in evacuation can kill. When you can get professional
help, get it.
